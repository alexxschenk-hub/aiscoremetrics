The NVIDIA DGX Spark promises an AI supercomputer for your desk: 128 GB of unified memory, a Blackwell GPU, CUDA – in a box the size of a Mac mini. At around €6,300 it competes directly with the Mac Studio. The question is: what are you paying the premium for?

What is the DGX Spark?

The DGX Spark uses the GB10 chip: an ARM CPU and a Blackwell GPU sharing 128 GB of LPDDR5X. About 119 GB of that is available to the model. The principle is similar to the Mac: because CPU and GPU share memory, models fit that won't fit on any consumer graphics card. There's also a 64 GB version for around €5,000 and equivalent machines from other vendors.

The catch is the memory bandwidth of 273 GB/s. That's less than an RTX 5060 Ti – and bandwidth decides how fast a model writes.

The Spark is slow at writing

The Mac Studio with M5 Max costs practically the same but has more than twice the bandwidth – so it writes about twice as fast with every model. The M5 Ultra is even three times as fast. With dense models like Llama 3.3 70B the gap is especially visible: 4 tok/s on the Spark is slower than you read.

Compared with the Ryzen AI Max+ 395, which has similar bandwidth, the Spark is barely faster at writing – yet costs almost twice as much.

Where the Spark shines

  • Prompt processing: thanks to Blackwell tensor cores, the Spark reads long prompts about twice as fast as the Ryzen AI Max+ and nearly as fast as the M5 Max.
  • CUDA: everything that runs on NVIDIA servers – vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, PyTorch, fine-tuning tools – runs here too. For developers who later deploy to DGX or cloud hardware, that's the real reason to buy.
  • Memory: 119 GB usable without changing any limit – a bit more than a 128 GB Mac Studio by default.
  • Clustering: two Sparks can be linked via the built-in high-speed network card to load even larger models.

Is the premium worth it?

Compared with the Ryzen AI Max+ 395 you pay about €2,900 more – essentially for CUDA and faster prompt processing. Compared with the Mac Studio M5 Max you pay the same but get only half the writing speed.

Verdict

DGX Spark if you develop AI software, want to fine-tune models or depend on the NVIDIA ecosystem. Mac Studio if you simply want to use large models as fast as possible – for the same money it writes twice as fast. Ryzen AI Max+ 395 if you want 128 GB at the lowest price.
